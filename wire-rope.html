In the lifting and rigging industries, wire rope is attached to a crane or hoist and fitted with swivels, shackles or hooks to attach to a load and move it in a controlled matter.

Four Basic Components

2 Multi-wire strands laid around a core in a helical pattern

Frequently Asked Questions

Find quick answers to common technical queries regarding wire rope specifications and standards.

The ISI Code standard for General Engineering Wire Rope is IS 2266:2002. Our range of wire ropes is supplied adhering to Indian Standard quality testing guidelines.

A Fiber Core (FC) uses natural or synthetic polypropylene fibers, offering greater elasticity and shock absorption, but is more susceptible to crushing and not recommended for high heat. A Steel Core (IWRC/WSC) uses an independent wire rope or strand core, providing superior structural support, crushing resistance, and is recommended for operating environments exceeding 180° F.

The Breaking Load is always 5 Times Greater than the Safe Working Load (S.W.L.). This 5:1 design factor ensures maximum safety margin during heavy industrial hoisting operations.

Wire rope sizes generally start from 1 MM and extend up to 52 MM and 100 MM depending on customer load lifting requirements.

Available surface finishes include Zinc Coated (Galvanized), Zinc/Aluminum Alloy Coated (Mischmetal), Stainless Steel (AISI 316), and Unfinished Steel ("Bright" self-colored with heavy protective lubrication).

6×19 and 6×36 constructions are commonly used for General Engineering Purposes, crane rigging, hoist lines, winches, and wire rope slings throughout many industrial sectors.
